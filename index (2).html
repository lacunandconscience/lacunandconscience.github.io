About Me

I’m Lavanya Chaudhary. I’ve questioned everything growing up, especially topics related to the double standards of Indian society, the lack of equality between different castes and religions, and court verdicts that I found partial. My work focuses mainly on law, but I’m equally interested in the moral, social and psychological questions surrounding it. I use this journal to examine subjects I find difficult, overlooked or unresolved, particularly within criminal justice, human rights and gender.

About the Journal

As mentioned above, my curiosity has always led me to question difficult and overlooked subjects. The question I return to most is whether legality always produces justice. Through this journal, I intend to examine that question.

I created Lacuna & Conscience as a space to document not only judgments, but also the realities behind them—the actions that lead to a case, the consequences that follow it and the details society often overlooks. My research papers approach each subject through three connected elements: law, philosophy and psychology.

I chose the name Lacuna & Conscience because lacuna stands for something that’s missing. It represents what the law leaves unanswered—maybe an overlooked harm or a faulty judgment. Meanwhile, conscience is the part of me that keeps asking if something is right or not. Together, they represent my central question: does legality always produce justice?

That’s also why I chose the owl and iris for the logo. The owl represents wisdom. Its sharp senses and ability to see in the dark are what drew me to it. I chose the iris to represent justice and the importance of being fair when making decisions. For me, its petals stand for faith, valour and wisdom.

01 / LAW

Law & Justice

Each paper is grounded in reliable sources, with all sources credited throughout the research. I examine criminal processes, punishment, delays and legal gaps to understand how the law operates in practice and where it may fail to address the complete reality of a case.

02 / PHILOSOPHY

Philosophy

Philosophy is embedded throughout my research because I question every stage of the subject I am examining—the assumptions behind it, the reasoning used to justify it and the morality of its consequences. It allows me to look beyond what happened and ask why it happened, whether it should have happened and what justice should require.

03 / PSYCHOLOGY

Psychology

I examine the mind and morality of the perpetrator, alongside questions of responsibility and the victim’s trauma and dignity. This perspective considers the human consequences that legal outcomes may address, overlook or never fully repair.
