About the journal

I am interested in the places where legal rules end and harder questions begin.

I created Lacuna & Conscience as an independent space to research law beyond statutes and outcomes: how justice is experienced, how social expectations shape legal reality, what happens when institutions move too slowly, and where morality and legality stop agreeing.

My work is especially drawn to gender, criminal justice, human rights, legal gaps, social responsibility and the psychological consequences of law. This journal does not present certainty for its own sake. It follows difficult questions through legislation, cases, scholarship and competing arguments.

Editorial focus

Where the journal returns.

Three recurring lines of inquiry shape the work—not as fixed categories, but as different ways of asking who law protects, what it overlooks and what remains unresolved.

01 / LAW

Law & justice

Criminal process, delay, punishment, legal gaps and the distance between legality and justice.

02 / POWER

Society & power

Gender, family honour, social surveillance and the structures that decide whose freedom feels negotiable.

03 / PERSON

Mind & morality

Trauma, dignity, responsibility and the human consequences that legal outcomes cannot fully contain.
